Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Titusville

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

Our Titusville crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the Machine DownCancel the cycle, and pull the plug only when the floor and the outlet near it are dry. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • A failed water inlet valve that keeps filling the washer or dishwasher after it should stop.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Cabinet Damage

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Mold Behind Appliances

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Flooring and Subfloor

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.
