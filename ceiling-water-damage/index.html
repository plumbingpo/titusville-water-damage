Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Titusville

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

Covering the mark with paint is not a repair. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. The opening is kept as small as the damage allows, wet insulation comes out, joists and surrounding drywall are dried, and the area is patched, textured and painted to blend. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Stay out from under it and call.

Warning Signs

  • Ceiling texture crumbling off or changing color in one section
  • A smoke detector, register or fixture with water running from it
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • One room that smells damp even though nothing looks wet
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Marks Start Showing

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Day 3 to 7

Sagging Sets In

Softened gypsum loses its hold on the screws. Between the joists the surface dips, joints split apart and a musty smell arrives below.

Past one week

Risk of Collapse

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

Our Process

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Trace the Leak

We track the water back to where it starts using moisture meters and thermal imaging, checking the room or attic above. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Remove and Dry Out

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Drying equipment, meaning a dehumidifier and air movers, works on the framing and adjacent drywall, with antimicrobial treatment for water that was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Sudden Collapse

Once saturated, drywall weighs much more and no longer holds its screws well. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

Damage on the Floor Above

The structure on the upper side, attic boards or the floor of the next story, tends to be wet too. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.

Wet Lights and Wiring

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

Water Travels Sideways

Water follows joists, pipes and ducts, so it can drip several feet from where it entered. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.
