No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Titusville Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map

Common Structural Drying Questions

For most homes, structural drying runs 3 to 5 days. The schedule depends on how saturated things are, which materials got wet, the ambient conditions and how the building is constructed. When saturation is heavy or the property is commercial, expect 5 to 7 days or beyond. Daily monitoring lets us keep you informed about the timeline.
Psychrometry is the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. IICRC-certified technicians on our staff apply it when deciding on temperature, airflow and the position of equipment. No two materials release moisture at the same rate, so the approach varies. The math helps us avoid leftover moisture that feeds mold as well as wood damage from drying too far.
Placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage. Our LGR dehumidifiers take 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a home model. High-velocity air movers also create the precise airflow needed to speed evaporation from structural surfaces. Without meters, there is also no way to confirm materials are actually dry.
Expect higher power consumption for as long as the drying equipment is in your home. The added cost is typically $50 to $150 for the entire drying period. It is usually reimbursable through your insurance claim as part of the restoration costs, depending on your policy.
Moisture content in every affected material is measured with calibrated meters. The job is done once those readings sit inside the acceptable range under IICRC S500 and match dry reference materials from unaffected parts of the building. We never rely on touch or appearance alone.

When to Call for Structural Drying in Titusville

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.
