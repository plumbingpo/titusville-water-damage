How to Prevent Storm Damage Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Photograph your property once a year so its condition is on record for insurance purposes
  • Schedule a roof inspection every year so weak spots are repaired before storm season
  • Extend downspouts and slope the soil so runoff moves away from the foundation
  • Learn where the main water shutoff is and how to close it if pipes are damaged in a storm
  • Keep gutters and downspouts clear so water drains properly in heavy rain
  • Cut back limbs that hang over or close to the house so wind cannot throw them into it

Warning Signs for Storm Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Flashing that is bent, loose or missing at roof edges, valleys and vents
  • Gutters or downspouts torn loose, bent or dumping water against the house
  • Bubbling paint and soft, drooping spots overhead once the rain stops
  • Water in the lowest level after storm drains back up or overflow
  • After a weather event, insulation inside walls or overhead feels soaked
  • Shingles that are gone, cracked or curled up when you look from the yard
  • Rooms that start to smell stale or moldy following a storm

What a Professional Brings to Storm Damage Repair

Storm damage creates complex, connected repair needs that take experienced restoration professionals to handle properly. Wet insulation, soaked roof decking and damp wall cavities often sit behind a leak that appears small, and without proper detection and drying they bring mold and a weakening structure. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace water paths and identify every affected area.

Emergency board-up and tarping also take proper technique to be effective. An improperly installed tarp can create new problems by channeling water or failing under wind load. We have done a lot of storm mitigation, and we use suitable materials and methods so the temporary protection holds until permanent repairs are on the calendar.
