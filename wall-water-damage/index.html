Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Titusville

Think of drywall as a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Signs to Look For

  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Carpet edges or floorboards wet and discolored right next to the wall
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Mold Inside Cavities

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. You can have a clean looking painted surface with heavy growth right behind it.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

Our Process

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.
