Wet Carpet Drying in Titusville

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. For Titusville homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
  • A rust colored line at the wall edge, a sign of wet tack strip
  • A squishy feel under your feet, or wet socks after walking across the room
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Subfloor Damage

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

Furniture Marks

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.
