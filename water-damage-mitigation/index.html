Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. It is not the repair. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. Our Titusville crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. Each is its own phase of the job. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Containment, using plastic barriers and controlled airflow so moisture and contaminants stay out of clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Before heavy rain returns, caulk openings at doors, windows and roof penetrations.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation of the house.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Leave the heat on during winter trips so the plumbing cannot freeze.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.

How Our Water Damage Mitigation Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Titusville Restoration:

1

Control the Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

We extract standing water using pumps and extraction units, then check carpet and its padding. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Plastic barriers and controlled airflow separate wet rooms from dry ones. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. Each visit includes logged moisture readings, and the structure is usually ready for repair work within 3 to 5 days.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
  • Windows keep fogging and the air feels humid days after the water loss.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • Underfoot the carpet is spongy because the padding below holds water.
  • After a leak, the baseboards have swollen or started separating from the wall.
  • A musty odor has begun because materials stayed wet for a day.
  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
